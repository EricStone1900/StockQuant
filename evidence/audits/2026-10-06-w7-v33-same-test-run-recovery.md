The cross-service smoke created separate isolated normal, recovery, and rejection Platform TestRuns. On recovery TestRun `2084e649-bab9-4b95-ae60-604a12db1d03`, Platform reserved and dispatched two rounds. Round 1 ran in the fixed `sha256:8cd6db4ae88cab7a1fb485613a67fb15a4d4e08460c28deb81510c1fa1861dfb` Runner image with network disabled. The first Runner Host persisted checkpoint sequence 1 (SHA-256 `19db4286f66ee3f31ebb53ec056c964d8d3b7d723c6d5bfbc9bcd9c55f4fa5e0`) and was killed with SIGKILL under fence 1. After its persisted lease expired, a second Host took over at fence 2, restored the same checkpoint hash, wrote checkpoint sequence 2 (cursor 2), and completed round 1. Round 2 then completed under the same TestRun. A stale-fence publication returned HTTP 409. The Experiment ledger settled exactly 46 of 80 cents (23 cents per round); the normal TestRun also completed both rounds and verified the frozen output metrics.

The rejection TestRun `bcb927ff-0941-4b94-911a-108119ae71b8` received the expected 422 budget-cap refusal, with zero Experiment allocations, model-call rows, or Runner Jobs. External model/provider calls were zero; active services were not connected, and temporary databases, containers, and networks were cleaned by the harness.

The Runner Host previously placed every round of one Experiment in the same `TestRun/Experiment/fence-N` directory, which made consecutive rounds collide with `EEXIST`. The workspace is now scoped by TestRun, Experiment, round, and fencing token. The recovery Fixture waits 45 seconds after its initial checkpoint to allow lease expiry and 12 seconds after the resumed checkpoint so the Host heartbeat durably writes sequence 2.
